Agent-Based Modeling of Traffic Congestion on Road Networks

How does free-flowing traffic tip into a jam? We simulate individual vehicles on a highway and on a real city network, locate the critical density at which free flow breaks down, and test how planning changes shift it.

Read the full overviewOpen the repository

Overview

Each driver reacts only to the car ahead, yet together they produce queues, stop-and-go waves and a drop in capacity. We treat the switch from free flow to congestion as a nonequilibrium phase transition and study it at two scales.

Stage 1 · Uninterrupted flow

Highway

A single-lane ring road in SUMO with no traffic lights. Three vehicle models, a density sweep, and a single bottleneck to separate spontaneous jams from bottleneck-induced ones.

Stages 2 & 3 · Interrupted flow

City network

A real urban district from OpenStreetMap with intersections, signals and speed limits. A network-density sweep, then planning changes such as closing a road or adding a link.

Research questions

RQ1 · Stage 1

Phase transition on a highway

What is the critical density on a road without traffic lights, and does the transition behave like a phase transition? How does it depend on the model, randomness and system size, and does it match real highway data?

RQ2 · Stage 2

Network-level critical point

Does a city network have a density at which total throughput peaks and then falls? Do local jams merge into large congested regions, and how does network structure decide where congestion starts?

RQ3 · Stage 3

Planning shifts the critical point

Do road closures or new links raise or lower the network's critical density and maximum flow? Can adding a road make things worse (Braess's paradox)?

Model equations

NaSch

\[\begin{aligned} v_i &\leftarrow \min(v_i+1,\ v_{\max}) \\ v_i &\leftarrow \min(v_i,\ g_i) \\ v_i &\leftarrow \max(v_i-1,\ 0) \\ x_i &\leftarrow x_i+v_i \end{aligned}\]

Accelerate, keep a safe gap, slow down at random with probability \(p\) (third line), then move. \(g_i\): empty cells ahead. All vehicles update in parallel.

Explore this model →

OVM

\[\frac{dv_i}{dt}=a\left[V(\Delta x_i)-v_i\right]\]\[V(h)=\frac{v_{\max}}{2}\left[\tanh(h-h_c)+\tanh h_c\right]\]

\(\Delta x_i\): headway to the car ahead; \(a\): sensitivity; \(h_c\): safety distance. Uniform flow becomes unstable when \(V'(h) > a/2\).

Explore this model →

IDM

\[\frac{dv_i}{dt}=a\left[1-\left(\frac{v_i}{v_0}\right)^{\delta}-\left(\frac{s^*(v_i,\Delta v_i)}{s_i}\right)^{2}\right]\]\[s^*(v,\Delta v)=s_0+\max\!\left(0,\ vT+\frac{v\,\Delta v}{2\sqrt{ab}}\right)\]

\(s_i\): gap to the car ahead; \(\Delta v_i\): approach rate; \(v_0,\ T,\ s_0,\ a,\ b,\ \delta\): desired speed, time gap, minimum gap, maximum acceleration, comfortable deceleration and exponent.

How we find the critical point

Order parameterThe share of jammed vehicles: near zero in free flow, rising sharply past the critical density.
\(\phi = N_{\text{jam}}/N\)
FluctuationsThe variance of the order parameter, or of the flow, peaks at the transition.
\(\chi = N\left(\langle\phi^2\rangle-\langle\phi\rangle^2\right)\)
Finite sizeRepeat on longer roads; a true transition gets sharper.
\(L_1 < L_2 < L_3\)
MFD peakIn the city network, the density at which total flow stops increasing.
\(k_c = \arg\max_k\, q(k)\)
