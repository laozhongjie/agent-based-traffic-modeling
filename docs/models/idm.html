Intelligent Driver Model (IDM)

A car accelerates toward its desired speed while adapting to the gap and the speed of its leader. The model combines free-road driving, following and anticipatory braking in one acceleration law.

This page explains the model and its assumptions. Examples are illustrative; they are not results from the project's formal SUMO runs.

1. Basic idea

Developed by Treiber, Hennecke and Helbing and presented in 2000, IDM is a continuous car-following model. The driver balances the wish to reach a preferred speed against the need to maintain a speed-dependent gap.

“Intelligent” refers to this adaptive driving rule. The model contains no learning algorithm or route optimization. The basic homogeneous model is deterministic; variability can be introduced through driver parameters or external processes.

2. State and closing-speed convention

The acceleration depends on own speed \(v_i\), net bumper-to-bumper gap \(s_i\), and closing speed \(\Delta v_i=v_i-v_{i+1}\). Positive \(\Delta v_i\) means the follower is approaching its leader.

For identical vehicle length \(\ell_{\mathrm{veh}}\), \(s_i=x_{i+1}-x_i-\ell_{\mathrm{veh}}\). The sign of closing speed matters: reversing it without changing the equation would reverse the anticipatory braking response.

3. Acceleration and desired gap

\[\frac{dx_i}{dt}=v_i,\qquad \frac{dv_i}{dt}=a\left[1-\left(\frac{v_i}{v_0}\right)^\delta-\left(\frac{s^*(v_i,\Delta v_i)}{s_i}\right)^2\right]\] \[s^*(v,\Delta v)=s_0+\max\left(0,vT+\frac{v\Delta v}{2\sqrt{ab}}\right)\]

The free-road term \(1-(v/v_0)^\delta\) accelerates a slow car toward \(v_0\). The interaction term \(-(s^*/s)^2\) reduces acceleration or produces braking when the available gap is small relative to the desired gap.

The desired gap contains a standstill distance \(s_0\), a time-headway contribution \(vT\), and a closing-speed contribution \(v\Delta v/(2\sqrt{ab})\). The nonnegative clamp shown here is a common implementation convention that keeps \(s^*\ge s_0\); record the exact variant used in SUMO.

Illustration: \(s_0=2\) m, \(T=1.5\) s, \(a=1\) m/s² and \(b=2\) m/s². At \(v=10\) m/s with \(\Delta v=0\), the desired gap is 17 m. If \(\Delta v=2\) m/s, it increases to about 24.1 m. These values illustrate the formula and are not a project calibration.

4. Parameters and physical units

QuantityMeaning and effect
\(v_0\)Desired free-road speed in m/s. It can differ between vehicles.
\(T\)Desired time headway in s. Larger \(T\) increases following distance at a given speed; it is not an explicit reaction delay.
\(s_0\)Minimum desired standstill gap in m.
\(a\)Maximum free-road acceleration parameter in m/s². It sets how quickly a slow car speeds up.
\(b\)Comfortable deceleration parameter in m/s², taken as positive. It shapes anticipatory braking and is not a hard maximum braking bound.
\(\delta\)Dimensionless acceleration exponent, commonly 4. It controls the approach to the desired speed.

5. Equilibrium and traffic waves

In uniform traffic, all cars have equal speed \(v<v_0\), so \(\Delta v=0\) and acceleration vanishes. The equilibrium net gap for the equation above is:

\[s_{\mathrm{eq}}(v)=\frac{s_0+vT}{\sqrt{1-(v/v_0)^\delta}},\qquad k(v)=\frac{1000}{s_{\mathrm{eq}}(v)+\ell_{\mathrm{veh}}}\]

With gaps and vehicle length in m, \(k\) is in veh/km. Equilibrium flow in veh/h is \(q=3.6kv\) when \(v\) is in m/s. The equation describes homogeneous equilibrium states; observed congested traffic need not stay on that curve.

Depending on parameters and density, disturbances may decay or amplify into stop-and-go waves. Closing-speed feedback supplies anticipatory braking, but does not make every state stable. Stability depends on derivatives of the full acceleration function with respect to speed, gap and closing speed; the OVM threshold cannot simply be reused.

6. Strengths and limitations

  • Parameters have direct physical meanings, and one smooth law spans free flow, following and braking.
  • Closing-speed feedback supports early braking when approaching a slower leader.
  • Collision avoidance is an intended property under suitable continuous dynamics and initial conditions; it is not an unconditional guarantee for arbitrary numerical steps, cut-ins or sudden stops.
  • The basic model does not include lane changes, explicit reaction delay or route choice. SUMO adds separate mechanisms for these tasks.

7. Role in this project

IDM is the main city-network car-following model and a continuous benchmark on the ring road. Compare its flow and jam patterns with NaSch and OVM, then keep driver parameters fixed while changing network planning scenarios.

SUMO provides an IDM implementation. Record its version, parameter mapping, internal integration step and safety settings; distinguish the car-following rule from signals, junction priorities and lane-changing logic.
