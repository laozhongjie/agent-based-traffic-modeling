Intelligent Driver Model (IDM)

Each car accelerates towards its desired speed and brakes when the gap is smaller than the gap it wants, a gap that grows with its speed and with how fast it is closing in. One smooth formula covers free driving, following and braking. Cars do not collide, but uniform flow can still break down.

The rule

Positions and speeds are continuous. A car looks at its speed \(v\), its net gap \(s\) to the car ahead, and its closing speed \(\Delta v=v-v_{\text{leader}}\) (positive when it is catching up):

\[\frac{dv}{dt}=a\left[1-\Bigl(\frac{v}{v_0}\Bigr)^{\delta}-\Bigl(\frac{s^*(v,\Delta v)}{s}\Bigr)^{2}\right],\qquad s^*=s_0+\max\Bigl(0,\ vT+\frac{v\,\Delta v}{2\sqrt{ab}}\Bigr)\]
  • Free-road term \(a[1-(v/v_0)^\delta]\): on an empty road the car accelerates at about \(a\) and levels off at its desired speed \(v_0\).
  • Interaction term \(-a(s^*/s)^2\): braking when the actual gap \(s\) is smaller than the desired gap \(s^*\), and almost nothing when it is much larger.
  • Desired gap \(s^*\): \(s_0\) when stopped, plus \(T\) seconds of driving at speed \(v\), plus a term that grows when the car is closing in. That last term makes the car brake early when it approaches a slower car.

The closing speed is the difference from OVM: an IDM car reacts as soon as its leader slows, not only once the gap has shrunk. Approaching gently, it brakes at about the comfortable deceleration \(b\); in an emergency it brakes harder. This is why IDM cars do not collide.

In uniform flow \(\Delta v=0\) and the acceleration is zero, which gives the equilibrium gap for each speed: \(s_e(v)=(s_0+vT)/\sqrt{1-(v/v_0)^\delta}\). At low speed this is about \(s_0+vT\): the gap grows linearly with speed, and \(T\) is the time headway.

What to look for

  1. Low density: a stopped car is absorbed

    At 20 veh/km the outlined car stops at 10 s. The cars behind brake early and gently, the stopped car pulls away at about \(a\), and the disturbance fades.

  2. Middle density: a wave that does not go away

    At 50 veh/km the same stop creates a jam that travels backwards at about 15 km/h, close to the 15–20 km/h of real highway jams, and never dissolves. Watch “Smallest gap”: it stays several metres; unlike OVM, IDM cars brake in time. (Without the stop, the 0.5 m starting jitter also grows into waves, but that takes several minutes.)

  3. Stronger acceleration: stable flow

    With \(a=2\ \mathrm{m/s^2}\) cars leave a jam fast enough to catch up with their leaders before the next car arrives: the disturbance fades and the unstable band disappears from the fundamental diagram. Drag \(a\) down to 0.5 and watch the band widen.

Why uniform flow can break down

Each gap \(s\) has an equilibrium speed \(v_e(s)\), the inverse of \(s_e(v)\). With density \(k=1000/(s+\ell)\) veh/km the uniform flow is \(q=3.6\,k\,v_e\) veh/h: the grey curve in the demo.

Whether it is stable can be decided for any car-following law \(dv/dt=f(s,v,\Delta v)\) from three partial derivatives at equilibrium. Long waves do not grow when (Wilson and Ward 2011)

\[f_s\le\frac{f_v^2}{2}+f_{\Delta v}\,f_v\]

\(f_s>0\) says how strongly a change in gap changes the acceleration; \(f_v^2/2\) is the damping from relaxing towards a speed; \(f_{\Delta v}f_v>0\) is the extra damping from reacting to the closing speed. For OVM, \(f=\alpha[V(s)-v]\) gives \(f_s=\alpha V'\), \(f_v=-\alpha\), \(f_{\Delta v}=0\), and the condition becomes \(V'\le\alpha/2\). IDM has the extra damping term, but with a small \(a\) or a short \(T\) the gap term still wins. The demo evaluates the condition numerically at every density to draw the band.

As with OVM, this is a long-wave criterion for small disturbances; a finite ring, a finite step and large disturbances (a stopped car) can all behave differently.

In SUMO

SUMO has IDM built in (carFollowModel="IDM"), with \(s_0\) given by minGap. Each step of length \(\Delta t\) is split into sub-steps of 0.25 s (4 for \(\Delta t=1\) s) during which the leader's speed is held fixed; then \(x'=x+\Delta t\,v'\). The demo does the same with \(\Delta t=1\) s, the step of our ring runs, and reproduces our SUMO ring sweep (results/ring/idm_sweep.csv) to about 1%.

The step length matters: with \(\Delta t=0.1\) s the same parameters are noticeably more stable. Our ring runs also set speedDev="0" and speedFactor="1" so that every car has the same \(v_0\).
