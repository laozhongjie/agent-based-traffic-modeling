Optimal Velocity Model (OVM)

A driver chooses a desired speed from the distance to the car ahead and gradually adjusts to it. Even with no random braking, small disturbances can grow when the response is too slow.

This page explains the model and its assumptions. Examples are illustrative; they are not results from the project's formal SUMO runs.

1. Basic idea

Introduced by Bando and colleagues in 1995, OVM models continuous positions and speeds. The optimal-velocity function \(V(h)\) assigns a desired speed to each headway: close vehicles prefer low speed; widely spaced vehicles approach a free-flow speed.

“Optimal” names the prescribed speed function. It does not mean each driver solves a route or travel-time optimization problem.

2. State and distance conventions

Let \(x_i(t)\) and \(v_i(t)\) be position and speed. Headway \(h_i=x_{i+1}-x_i\) is the distance between corresponding points on successive vehicles. On a ring, it is measured forward around the boundary.

Headway is not the empty bumper-to-bumper gap. For identical vehicle length \(\ell_{\mathrm{veh}}\), that gap is \(s_i=h_i-\ell_{\mathrm{veh}}\). Keep this distinction when translating an analytical OVM into SUMO.

3. Relaxation toward the desired speed

\[\frac{dx_i}{dt}=v_i,\qquad \frac{dv_i}{dt}=\alpha[V(h_i)-v_i]\]

If a car is slower than its desired speed, it accelerates. If it is faster, it decelerates. The sensitivity \(\alpha>0\) has units s\(^{-1}\), and \(1/\alpha\) is the relaxation time, not an explicit reaction delay.

A dimensionally consistent version of a common smooth function is:

\[V(h)=\frac{v_{\max}}{1+\tanh(h_c/d)}\left[\tanh\left(\frac{h-h_c}{d}\right)+\tanh\left(\frac{h_c}{d}\right)\right]\]

This choice gives \(V(0)=0\) and \(V(h)\to v_{\max}\) for large headway. The homepage's shorter \(v_{\max}/2\) form is often written in scaled coordinates; its parameter is an amplitude, with large-headway limit \(v_{\max}[1+\tanh(h_c)]/2\). Fix the length scaling and normalization before a quantitative comparison.

4. Parameters and physical units

QuantityMeaning and effect
\(\alpha\)Sensitivity in s\(^{-1}\). A larger value gives faster relaxation toward \(V(h)\).
\(v_{\max}\)Asymptotic desired speed in m/s for the normalized function above.
\(h_c,\ d\)Transition headway and smoothing length in m. \(h_c\) locates the steepest part of the curve; smaller \(d\) makes the speed response to headway steeper.
\(h_0=L/N\)Uniform headway in m on a ring of physical length \(L\) with \(N\) vehicles. Density in veh/km is \(1000/h_0\).
\(\Delta t\)Numerical integration step in s, not a defining OVM parameter. Check convergence as the step is reduced.

5. Uniform flow and instability

In uniform flow, every headway equals \(h_0\) and every speed equals \(V(h_0)\). Linearizing around this state gives, for the standard homogeneous OVM and long-wavelength disturbances on a large ring, the stability condition \(V'(h_0)<\alpha/2\). The boundary is \(V'(h_0)=\alpha/2\).

\[V'(h)=\frac{v_{\max}}{d[1+\tanh(h_c/d)]}\operatorname{sech}^2\left(\frac{h-h_c}{d}\right)\]

When \(V'(h_0)>\alpha/2\), a small change in headway demands a large change in desired speed, and delayed relaxation can amplify the disturbance. Cars alternately brake and accelerate, generating stop-and-go waves without stochastic forcing.

This criterion depends on the model and background state. A finite ring has discrete perturbation modes, so its threshold may differ from the long-wavelength limit. Nonlinear jams and numerical safeguards also require separate checks.

6. Strengths and limitations

  • A compact mechanism with an analytical stability condition: useful for separating deterministic instability from random noise.
  • The basic acceleration law uses headway and own speed, with no separate closing-speed term.
  • It does not guarantee collision avoidance or realistic acceleration bounds. Extreme conditions and numerical integration can produce unphysical states.
  • Any clipping, emergency braking or safety override changes the simulated dynamics and must be documented.

7. Role in this project

OVM supplies the deterministic theory reference. Perturb uniform ring-road flow slightly, sweep density and sensitivity, and compare perturbation growth with the analytical prediction.

The project plans a C++ implementation in the fixed SUMO build. Verify the exact \(V(h)\), headway definition, integrator and safety interventions before comparing it with the theoretical model.
