Nagel–Schreckenberg model (NaSch)

The road is a row of 7.5 m cells and time moves in 1 s steps. Four rules update every car at once. The only randomness, an occasional slowdown, is enough to make traffic jam with no bottleneck at all.

The rule

Car \(i\) sits in cell \(x_i\) and has an integer speed \(v_i\in\{0,\ldots,v_{\max}\}\) in cells per step. Its gap \(g_i=x_{i+1}-x_i-1\) is the number of empty cells to the car ahead. Every step, all cars apply four rules together:

  1. Accelerate: \(v\leftarrow\min(v+1,\,v_{\max})\). Drivers want to go faster, by at most one cell per step each step.
  2. Don't hit the car ahead: \(v\leftarrow\min(v,\,g)\). A car never moves past the cell behind its leader's current position. This is the only interaction, and it uses the gap alone, not the leader's speed.
  3. Slow down at random: with probability \(p\), \(v\leftarrow\max(v-1,\,0)\). This stands for everything imperfect about drivers: distraction, overbraking, a slow start.
  4. Move: \(x\leftarrow x+v\).

Rule 2 makes collisions impossible: even if the leader does not move this step, the follower ends up behind it. Rule 2 is also very cautious, since it plans for a leader that might stop dead. All cars update from the same old state; updating them one by one, with each car seeing its leader's new position, would let platoons move like trains and change the results.

What to look for

  1. No randomness, low density: free flow

    With \(p=0\) and 15 veh/km every car soon has at least \(v_{\max}\) empty cells ahead, so rule 2 never applies and every car drives at \(v_{\max}\). The current point sits on the rising line \(q=v_{\max}\rho\).

  2. Random slowdowns: phantom jams

    At 20 veh/km, which is free flow when \(p=0\), set \(p=0.25\). Now and then a car slows at random, the car behind has to slow too, and so on: short dark bands appear in the space–time diagram, travel backwards and dissolve. Nothing blocks the road.

  3. High density: jams that persist

    At 45 veh/km the jams no longer dissolve; they drift backwards steadily. Follow the outlined car: it stops in a jam, leaves it, speeds up and meets the next one. Cars move forwards through a jam while the jam itself moves backwards: a jam is a wave, not a fixed group of cars.

Why the fundamental diagram is a triangle

On a ring of \(L\) cells with \(N\) cars, the occupancy is \(\rho=N/L\) and the flow past any point is \(q=\frac{1}{L}\sum_i v_i\) cars per step. Two bounds follow straight from the rules:

  • no car is faster than \(v_{\max}\), so \(q\le v_{\max}\rho\);
  • no car moves further than its gap, and the gaps add up to the \(L-N\) empty cells, so \(q\le1-\rho\).

With \(p=0\) the flow reaches whichever bound is lower:

\[q=\min\bigl(v_{\max}\rho,\ 1-\rho\bigr),\qquad \rho_c=\frac{1}{v_{\max}+1}\]

At \(\rho_c\) each car plus its \(v_{\max}\) empty cells exactly fills the road. Below it, everyone drives at \(v_{\max}\); above it, the flow is limited by the empty cells and falls as cars are added. In physical units (\(\ell=7.5\) m, \(\Delta t=1\) s, \(v_{\max}=5\)), \(\rho_c\) is 22 veh/km and the peak flow 3000 veh/h, more than a real lane carries, because \(p=0\) cars follow with no reaction time.

With \(p>0\) there is no exact result. Random slowdowns lower the free speed to about \(v_{\max}-p\) and keep creating small disturbances that rule 2 passes backwards. The peak flow drops, moves to a lower density and is rounded off: there is no longer a sharp threshold. Whether the transition still behaves like a phase transition is what our ring-road experiment tests, through the fluctuations of the jammed fraction on rings of different lengths.

In SUMO

SUMO 1.28.0 has NaSch built in (carFollowModel="NaSch"). It applies the same four rules on SUMO's continuous road: speeds are rounded down to whole cells per step, the next speed is limited by the gap alone, and sigma is the slowdown probability. The cell length comes from accel: one step of acceleration covers one cell.

With the settings above it matches the original model: at \(p=0\) the mean speed equals the exact result (checked in sumo/version.txt). Two things break this: cars that do not start on cell positions, and speedDev or speedFactor left at SUMO's defaults, which give every car its own top speed.
